Keeping Your Food Fresh on the Way Home

Good takeout depends on what happens during the ride. Keep the bag flat and upright, and avoid placing it in a hot trunk or on a sloped seat. If your curry and rice are packed separately, keep the containers level so sauces do not spill.

When you reach home, open the containers right away. Trapped steam softens breads and makes crispy items soggy, so loosen the lids for a few minutes. Plan to eat or refrigerate your food soon after you arrive, since food should not sit at room temperature for more than two hours.

Reheating Without Drying Out Your Meal

Curries and Stews

Transfer curries to a saucepan and warm them over low to medium heat. Stir often, and add a splash of water if the sauce has thickened in the fridge. Heat until the dish is steaming throughout, not just on the edges.

Biryani and Rice

Sprinkle a tablespoon of water over the rice, cover the pan, and heat on low. Gently fluff the grains with a fork once they are warm. This keeps the grains separate and prevents dry spots.

Breads and Tandoor Dishes

Wrap naan or roti loosely in foil and warm it in an oven until heated through. For tandoor dishes, a short time in a hot skillet or oven restores some of the char without overcooking the meat or paneer.

Storing Leftovers Safely

Divide leftovers into shallow containers so they cool quickly. Refrigerate them promptly, and label each container with the date. Most leftovers stay good for a few days in the fridge. If you will not eat them within that window, freeze curries and biryani in portions, then thaw them overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.
